HEALTH / FICTIONAL EXERCISE

When “beds added” becomes “care improved”

In this fictional exercise, a hospital announces 100 additional beds. Staffing and occupancy data are absent. The announcement supports a capacity claim; it does not establish better access or outcomes. No real hospital or publisher is assessed.

ENVIRONMENT / FICTIONAL EXERCISE

One rainfall figure, two different baselines

A fictional district records 120 mm of rain. A 100 mm monthly baseline makes that 20% above average; a 150 mm seasonal comparison makes it 20% below. These comparisons answer different questions. Neither establishes a trend.

Who gets to describe the impact?

In a fictional sample of 12 articles collected over 48 hours, 3 quote affected residents. Nine do not. This describes the sample, not the whole Indian media landscape.

HOW TO READ THIS / THE DENOMINATOR TEST

“Up 40%” compared with what?

Find the starting number, time period and population. Then check whether the headline and the evidence use the same comparison. Try this on a post from your own feed.
