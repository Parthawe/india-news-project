OUR METHOD / VERSION 1.0

Show the work behind the assessment.

We examine how a story is built, which claims survive checking, and where the evidence ends. No single score can answer all three.

Coverage gaps

The blindspot method

A political blindspot is a finding about uneven story coverage in a defined sample. It is separate from an assessment of one article’s framing. The reviewer currently uses reader-added links; automated monitoring and reviewed outlet political ratings are unavailable. It therefore makes no political blindspot finding.

The reviewer counts distinct website sources, unavailable material and unexamined YouTube transcripts. These describe limits of the submitted sample. Few links do not establish that a political side or an entire regional media landscape ignored a story.

Before a finding can be published

  1. An editor confirms that the articles and broadcasts concern the same event.
  2. The monitored source list and the start and end of the observation window are disclosed.
  3. Outlet assessments include evidence, review dates and review status. Government alignment stays separate from political leaning.
  4. Distinct outlets are counted once; copied wire reports are identified.
  5. The full sample, rated and unassessed sources, extraction failures and language limitations stay visible.
  6. Published selection rules and editorial review precede any finding, which must be scoped to the monitored sample.

A TV website article does not stand for everything a channel broadcast. YouTube titles and uploader metadata do not establish transcript content or broadcast coverage.

Ground News’s blindspot explanation · Inspect your own comparison

01

Evidence

What can we establish?

Separate verified facts, attributed claims, conflicting evidence and unanswered questions. A confident headline is not verification.

02

Framing

How is responsibility presented?

Compare verbs, emphasis, causal claims and the choice of headline. Describe the article before generalising about its publisher.

03

Representation

Who gets to speak?

Identify quoted institutions, affected people, experts and absent perspectives. Explain why a missing voice could matter.

04

Context

What changes the comparison?

Check history, geography, time windows, denominators and qualifications. Translate faithfully and retain the original wording.

05

Ownership & funding

Which relationships are documented?

Show dated ownership records and disclosures. Ownership alone does not establish editorial interference; an absent label does not establish an unpaid post.

06

Connections

Where did the claim begin?

Trace original reporting, wire copies, quotes and reused media. Shared facts alone are not evidence of coordination.

Assessments must be traceable.

Political leaning, government alignment and reporting practices are assessed separately, with a dated rationale and examples. Unassessed is a valid result. AI drafts do not become published assessments without an editor.

A coverage gap is a count within named sources, languages and dates. We distinguish article counts from independent reporting origins and never turn an unrepresentative sample into a claim about all Indian news.

English and Hindi editions share claim and evidence IDs. Translators preserve attribution, uncertainty and quantities; originals remain available beside translated headlines. Text changes invalidate earlier reviews.

When we get it wrong

Corrections are reviewed against the original evidence. Published changes retain the earlier version, date, reviewer and reason. Ownership and sponsorship findings require evidence; readers can challenge them.
